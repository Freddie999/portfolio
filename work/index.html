01

Mapping / Academic project

Rwanda Healthcare Access Explorer↗

An exploratory dashboard linking modeled walking times to healthcare facilities with estimated population across Rwanda.

  • JavaScript
  • MapLibre GL JS
  • PMTiles
  • H3
Contribution
Dashboard design and implementation, spatial-data preparation and integration, vector-tile mapping, filtering, linked interactions, and source documentation.
Evidence
A deployed interactive application with three facility types, adjustable walking-time thresholds, linked area selection, population-ranked results, and reset and empty-result states. Summary totals cover all matching cells in the dataset, including those outside the current map view.
Scope
An academic, exploratory tool for shortlisting areas for further investigation. Walking times are modeled, not observed patient journeys; the source no_sites scenario meaning, data dates, and reuse terms remain unconfirmed. Population totals are estimates, cells are not clipped to Rwanda’s border, and zero travel times are excluded as uncertain. Population ranking is not an intervention-priority score or a validated facility-location recommendation.

03

Machine learning / Academic project

Reading the landscape↗

Testing what actually improves a satellite-image classifier: deeper features, augmentation, skip connections, and dropout.

  • Python
  • PyTorch
  • CNN
Contribution
Experimental comparisons, validation-based selection, evaluation, and error analysis.
Evidence
The selected augmented plain CNN classified 3,958 of 4,050 held-out images correctly: 97.73% accuracy and 0.9767 macro F1. Skip connections and dropout did not improve validation accuracy in this run.
Scope
One seed and a random image split. Similar or nearby scenes can cross partitions, so the score does not establish performance in a new region. Adapted from Xiaojiang Li’s course notebook.

05

Policy / Academic project

Verify the poverty claim↗

Checking claims about Philadelphia poverty against the uncertainty in American Community Survey estimates.

  • R
  • tidycensus
  • Quarto
Contribution
Data reconstruction, uncertainty calculations, visual explanation, and policy interpretation.
Evidence
Across 384 tracts: 36 significant increases, 27 significant decreases, 313 inconclusive changes, and eight unassessable comparisons. Tests use 90% confidence.
Scope
Individual tests are not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Tracts are not neighborhoods, and nonsignificance does not establish no change. These results do not establish a citywide or race-specific trend.
06

Policy / Team academic project

What shapes housing values?↗

A team study combining regression, geographic diagnostics, and cross-validation on historical Philadelphia housing data.

  • R
  • Regression
  • Quarto
Contribution
Shared team report with Vincent Bio Bediako and Emmanuel Sampson.
Evidence
The four-predictor model explains about 66% of variation in logged house values. Cross-validation RMSE was 0.366440 log units versus 0.442722 for the vacancy-and-income model, a 17.2% reduction.
Scope
Historical, block-group-level associations do not establish causal effects or individual-house predictions. Spatially clustered errors can make random cross-validation optimistic. Team: Vincent Bio Bediako, Frederick Abalo, and Emmanuel Sampson; individual task allocation is not documented.

08

Mapping / Academic project

Philadelphia Shadow Simulation↗

A GPU-based ray-casting simulation of hourly shadows across a Philadelphia digital surface model.

  • Python
  • PyCUDA
  • Rasterio
  • NumPy
Contribution
Course-notebook adaptation, hourly scenario setup, unit and sun-direction handling, and output presentation.
Evidence
Ten hourly shadow maps from 08:00 to 17:00 EDT on September 15, 2025, plus an animation showing the changing shadow pattern. The notebook and exported images are available for inspection.
Scope
This is a deterministic spatial simulation, not machine learning: it has no model training or learned parameters. It adapts Xiaojiang Li’s course notebook and covers one DSM tile on one day. Vertical units are assumed because the input does not specify them, and objects outside the tile cannot cast shadows into it. No ground-truth accuracy assessment or CPU/GPU speed benchmark is reported.
09

Machine learning / Academic project

Predicting Philadelphia Poverty Rates↗

Comparing ACS baselines, spatial features, and transformations to see what improves tract-level poverty-rate prediction.

  • R
  • sf
  • tidycensus
  • caret
  • Linear regression
Contribution
Outcome and reliability assessment, data assembly, spatial feature engineering, model comparisons and transformations, residual mapping, and interpretation within the course-provided workflow.
Evidence
The selected log-density model had a reported ten-fold CV RMSE of 8.89 percentage points, compared with 9.05 for the ACS baseline—a modest improvement. Burglary density alone worsened RMSE to 9.09. The baseline and final in-sample residual maps still show geographic clusters of over- and under-prediction.
Scope
An academic predictive-modeling exercise, not a causal explanation of poverty or a tool for allocating services to individual tracts. ACS estimates have sampling uncertainty, and recorded incidents reflect reporting and enforcement patterns. Model selection and comparison reuse the same random CV folds; no independent test set or spatially blocked validation is reported. The small improvement may not generalize to geographically separate areas. Residual maps show in-sample errors, not held-out predictions.
